# Capability: co-authoring documents and internal communications

## Co-authoring specs, proposals, decision docs

Work in three stages.

1. **Gather context.** Ask the person to dump what they know in any order:
   goal, audience, constraints, background, open questions. Ask a few targeted
   follow-ups (at most five), then stop asking.
2. **Draft section by section.** For each section: list what belongs in it,
   write it, show it, take edits, move on. Keep the person's wording where
   it is good. Surface decisions and unknowns explicitly instead of papering over them.
3. **Reader test.** Predict three questions a cold reader would ask and check the
   document answers them. Cut anything that does not serve the reader.

Typical decision-doc skeleton: context, problem, options with trade-offs,
recommendation, risks, open questions, next steps.

## Status updates and internal comms

Choose the format by purpose.

- **Progress / 3P update**: Progress (what shipped), Plans (what is next),
  Problems (what is blocked and what you need). Few lines each, concrete, with numbers.
- **Leadership update**: the headline first, then the three facts that matter,
  then the ask.
- **Incident report**: summary, impact, timeline (timestamps), root cause,
  what fixed it, what changes to prevent recurrence. Blameless wording.
- **FAQ**: real questions in the audience's words, short answers, links for depth.
- **Newsletter**: scannable sections, one theme per item, a clear call to action.

## Style

- Plain language, active voice, specific nouns and numbers.
- Lead with the point; context after.
- One idea per paragraph; short sentences.
- Do not invent facts, dates, owners, or commitments. Mark gaps as TBD
  and tell the person what is missing.
