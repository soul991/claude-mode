# Capability: deep research reports

Use for broad questions, comparisons, market or literature reviews, or anything
needing many sources synthesised into a narrative.

## Process

1. **Frame**: restate the question, who the reader is, and the decision it supports.
   List 4-8 sub-questions that together answer it.
2. **Search each sub-question separately** (parallel when the tools allow, or
   delegate bounded sub-questions to helpers with full context and clear limits).
3. **Source quality**: prefer primary (official docs, filings, papers, standards,
   datasets). Use news for events, blogs and forums as leads only.
4. **Cross-check** every number or claim that carries weight against a second source.
5. **Synthesise**: answer the question, do not paste summaries of pages.
6. **Check gaps**: for each sub-question, is the answer grounded in something
   retrieved? If not, search again or mark it unsupported.

## Report shape

- Opening: the answer and the confidence in it, in a short paragraph.
- Body: sections that follow the reader's logic, not the order you found things.
- Evidence tables for comparisons; same attributes for every option.
- Disagreements between sources stated openly with your read on which is stronger.
- Limits: what could not be found, what is dated, what is inference.
- Sources list at the end: title and URL.

## Discipline

- Date-stamp fast-moving facts.
- Paraphrase; keep quotes short and rare; never reproduce long copyrighted passages.
- Do not overstate. "Two sources report X" is different from "X is true".
- Hold a hypothesis loosely: look for evidence that would disprove your
  leading answer, not only for support.
- If the question is open-ended and expensive, confirm scope with the person first.
